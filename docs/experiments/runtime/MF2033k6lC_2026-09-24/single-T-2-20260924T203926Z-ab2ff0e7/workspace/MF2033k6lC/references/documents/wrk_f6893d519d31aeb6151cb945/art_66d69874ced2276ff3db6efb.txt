Graph Continual Learning aims to enable models to acquire knowledge from sequentially arriving graph tasks while preserving performance on previously learned tasks. However, training on new tasks can overwrite learned representations and parameters, leading to catastrophic forgetting. Existing methods mainly mitigate forgetting through experience replay, regularization constraints, or parameter isolation, but they often lack an explicit mechanism for modeling selective forgetting. To address this issue, we propose Task-Correlation-Guided Memory Regulation (TCMR), which uses learnable task correlation as a unified control signal to jointly guide memory retention, replacement, and replay. In addition, TCMR introduces a correlation-weighted parameter consolidation mechanism to enable differentiated knowledge preservation and updating. Specifically, historical parameter anchors are protected according to task relevance, which helps prevent highly correlated old-task knowledge from being overwritten during long task sequences. Experiments under node-level continual learning with both task-incremental learning (Task-IL) and class-incremental learning (Class-IL) settings show that the proposed method effectively alleviates catastrophic forgetting and achieves competitive performance in most experimental settings.
