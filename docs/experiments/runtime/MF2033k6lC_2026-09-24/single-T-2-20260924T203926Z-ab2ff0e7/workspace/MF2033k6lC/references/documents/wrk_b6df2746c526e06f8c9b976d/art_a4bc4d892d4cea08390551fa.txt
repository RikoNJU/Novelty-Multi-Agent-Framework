The mechanism distinguishing visual subitizing (small n ) from estimation (large n ) is unknown. We propose a theoretical mechanism based on the dynamical stability of an On-Centre Off-Surround (OCOS) recurrent neural network. A linear stability analysis of the model reveals that the spectrum of modal decay rates ( $$\lambda _k$$ λ k ) is parametrically controlled by numerosity ( n ) and inhibition ( $$\beta $$ β ). We derive that for small n , the system is highly stable, causing all noise-driven fluctuations to decay rapidly. As n increases, the network is tuned towards a critical boundary. This “critical slowing down” creates a dense spectrum of slow modes, which theoretically generates persistent, long-range temporal correlations (LRTCs). The model thus predicts that subitizing should be a dynamically memoryless (white noise) process, while estimation should exhibit persistent LRTCs (pink noise). To evaluate this prediction, we re-analyzed reaction time (RT) series from a continuous enumeration task. The empirical results aligned with our theoretical derivation: Subitizing RTs were effectively uncorrelated ( $$\alpha _{\text {DFA}}= 0.498 \pm 0.083$$ α DFA = 0.498 ± 0.083 ), matching the prediction for a stable system. In contrast, estimation RTs exhibited a mean exponent characteristic of dynamical persistence ( $$\alpha _{\text {DFA}}= 0.674 \pm 0.087$$ α DFA = 0.674 ± 0.087 ). Although high inter-subject variability limited the statistical significance of this contrast, the dynamical persistence in the estimation regime was behaviorally relevant: subjects with higher $$\alpha _{\text {DFA}}$$ α DFA exhibited a larger RT switch cost when moving from estimation to subitizing ( $$r \approx 0.41$$ r ≈ 0.41 ). We conclude that the subitizing-estimation divide may reflect a fundamental transition in the dynamical stability of the underlying neural system.
