Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are a class of deep learning models that have demonstrated significant potential in various classification tasks, including image recognition and the prediction of labels for nodes and edges. Unlike traditional neural networks that operate on Euclidean data such as images or sequences, GNNs are specifically designed to handle non-Euclidean data, i.e. data represented as graphs, where relationships between entities are irregular and complex. These graphs may include rich node and edge attributes, making GNNs particularly suitable for domains such as social networks, molecular structures, and recommendation systems. Several GNN variants have been developed, including GCN, Cluster-GCN, GraphSAGE, and FastGCN. Despite their advantages, GNNs often require substantial memory, primarily due to the large adjacency matrices involved, which become computationally expensive for large-scale graphs. They also tend to have long training times. In this work, we propose a novel graph partitioning technique aimed at reducing the effective size of the graph while maintaining a high predictive F1-score. Furthermore, we integrate this partitioning approach with layer-wise training to enhance computational efficiency.
