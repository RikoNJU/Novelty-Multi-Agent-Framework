Abstract.

Real-time recommendation systems face the challenge of dynamically changing user interests and contextual environments. Traditional sequential recommendation models rely on static historical click sequences, which struggle to capture real-time preference shifts and ignore the structured information embedded in the system’s internal ranking logic. This paper proposes POEM (Partial-Order Enhanced Modeling), a novel real-time sequential modeling framework that leverages the partial-order relations inherent in the recommendation cascade. POEM innovatively utilizes the real-time multi-task ranking scores (e.g., predicted CTR, watch time) from the preceding ranking stage as supervisory signals to construct dynamic partial-order sequences, thereby achieving fine-grained real-time interest modeling and aligning system objectives with user behavior. Our contributions are threefold: a partial-order guided sequence construction paradigm that augments traditional temporal sequences with a dynamically grouped and sampled sequence based on real-time ranking scores, enabling per-request interest reassessment; (2) a multi-objective score fusion mechanism that integrates various ranking signals through normalized rank-weighting into a unified quintuple representation; and (3) a hierarchical sample learning strategy combining system-preferred items (top-ranked) and user feedback (e.g., long-play videos) as positive samples, enhanced by graph-retrieved hard negative samples and a margin-based pairwise loss.
Deployed in Kuaishou’s platform, POEM achieves significant online gains: +0.249% and +0.213% in average viewing time per user on the KS Single Page and KS Lite Page, respectively. Extensive ablation studies validate the effectiveness of each component, demonstrating POEM’s superiority in real-time responsiveness, recommendation accuracy, and content diversity.

Keywords: Personalized Retrieval, Partial-Order Learning, Real-Time Modeling, Ranking Signal Fusion, Sequential Recommendation

1. Introduction

The pursuit of real-time, precise user interest modeling lies at the heart of modern information-rich platforms, such as short-video feeds and e-commerce. In these dynamic environments, a user’s intent can shift rapidly, influenced by immediate context and the presented content itself. Sequential recommendation models have become a cornerstone technique by modeling user behavior over time. Models like SASRec (Kang and McAuley, 2018) and BERT4Rec (Sun et al., 2019), often built upon Transformer architectures, excel at capturing sequential dependencies within a user’s historical click sequence.

However, a fundamental disconnect exists between these models and the industrial recommendation pipeline. These models typically operate on a static snapshot of past clicks, treating the recommendation system as a mere observer. In reality, the system is an active participant: its intricate ranking stages continuously generate rich, multi-faceted estimations of user preference—such as predicted click-through rate (CTR), conversion rate (CVR), and watch time—for thousands of candidate items in real-time. These ranking signals form a partial order that reflects the system’s current, context-aware understanding of what the user might prefer next. This valuable source of supervisory signal remains largely untapped by traditional sequential recommenders, which rely solely on the observed user clicks, a signal that is sparse, delayed, and lacks the granular preference discrimination inherent in ranking scores.

This oversight leads to two key limitations: (1) Underutilization of System Intelligence: Valuable preference information from the ranking cascade is ignored, limiting model expressiveness. (2) Inherent Latency in Real-Time Modeling: Models depend on new user clicks to update their sequence view. In a fast-paced feed, a user’s interest might evolve based on unseen items or changing context long before a new click occurs, leaving the model with a stale representation.

To bridge this gap, we propose POEM (Partial-Order Enhanced Modeling), a novel framework that rethinks sequence construction for real-time recommendation. The core thesis of POEM is: the real-time ranking scores from the system can serve as a powerful supervisory signal to construct the input sequence itself, guiding the model towards learning a representation that mirrors the system’s current, preference-aware view of the user.

In POEM, for each user request, we dynamically construct a sequence not only from the user’s past clicks, but also from the top-ranked candidate items of the user’s previous refresh request. These items are sorted and grouped by their fused multi-objective ranking scores, and then randomly sampled within groups. This yields a partial-order structured sequence that encapsulates the system’s preference hierarchy. A special [C​L​S][CLS] token aggregates information from this sequence to form the user’s instantaneous interest embedding for retrieval. This approach ensures the user representation is re-evaluated on every request based on the freshest contextual signals, achieving true request-level real-time modeling.

To effectively learn from this novel sequence paradigm, we design a complementary hierarchical learning strategy. We define positive samples as a combination of system-predicted top-ranked items and actual user feedback (e.g., long-play videos), aligning the learning objective with both system efficiency and user satisfaction. Furthermore, we employ a dynamic, graph-based strategy to mine hard negative samples tailored to the current context, enhancing the model’s discriminative power for fine-grained partial-order learning.

We evaluate POEM comprehensively on large-scale industrial data from Kuaishou.
Online A/B tests demonstrate significant improvements across two major scenarios: +0.249% in average usage time per user on the KS Single Page and +0.213% on the KS Lite Page (see Table 5).
Rigorous offline ablation studies confirm the individual contribution of each component: the partial-order construction paradigm, multi-signal fusion, and the hierarchical learning strategy.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We propose a novel partial-order guided sequence construction framework for real-time recommendation. It dynamically builds user interest sequences from real-time multi-objective ranking scores, replacing the conventional reliance on static chronological click histories.

•

Within this framework, we introduce a multi-objective signal fusion mechanism to unify heterogeneous ranking signals, and design a hierarchical learning objective that aligns system predictions with genuine user feedback, supported by context-aware hard negative sampling.

•

We implement the proposed POEM framework end-to-end in a large-scale industrial platform and validate its effectiveness through rigorous offline analyses and online A/B tests, demonstrating significant gains in both recommendation accuracy and real-time responsiveness.

2. Related Work

2.1. Sequential Recommendation

Sequential recommendation focuses on predicting a user’s next interaction by capturing patterns in their behavior history. Early approaches were based on Markov Chains and RNNs (Rendle et al., 2010; Hidasi et al., 2015; Tang and Wang, 2018a). The introduction of the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) revolutionized the field, leading to models like SASRec (Kang and McAuley, 2018) and BERT4Rec (Sun et al., 2019) that model long-range dependencies. Recent advances include CL4SRec (Xie et al., 2022) which incorporates contrastive learning, and MPFormer (Sun et al., 2025a) which introduces a multi-task Transformer for heterogeneous patterns. Mao et al. (Mao et al., 2024) propose a dynamic user interest model to capture high-frequency preference shifts.

A core challenge remains the modeling of evolving user interests. Methods like DIN (Zhou et al., 2018), DIEN (Zhou et al., 2019), and MIMN (Lu et al., 2025) design specialized architectures to capture interest shifts over time, while SIM (Pi et al., 2020) retrieves relevant long-term behaviors. However, even these models operate on a static, chronologically ordered sequence of historical interactions, and their updates are triggered only by new observed feedback. In contrast, POEM dynamically constructs a sequence from real-time ranking scores, allowing the user representation to refresh on every request, even in the absence of new interactions.

2.2. Context-Aware Recommendation

Contextual information—such as time, location, and device—plays a crucial role in personalizing recommendations. A wide range of techniques have been developed to integrate such signals. Shallow models like Factorization Machines (Koren et al., 2009) efficiently model feature interactions. Deep learning-based architectures, including DeepFM (Guo et al., 2017) and xDeepFM (Lian et al., 2018), further enhance the capacity to learn high-order feature combinations. In sequential settings, context can be integrated through time-aware attention mechanisms (Li et al., 2020) or by attending to contextually relevant historical behaviors (Guo et al., 2025). These methods typically treat context as auxiliary features that are fused with user and item representations.

Cutting-edge research now focuses on sequence refinement. MGSD-WSS (Li et al., 2025) utilizes weakly supervised signals to denoise historical sequences, ensuring that only contextually relevant behaviors are preserved. However, these methods typically treat context as auxiliary features to be fused with representations. POEM leverages real-time multi-objective ranking scores as a more fundamental, intent-reflective context, using them to structurally guide the construction of the input sequence itself.

2.3. Utilizing Ranking Signals in Recommendation

Ranking signals, which have long been pivotal in the final re-ranking stage of recommender systems (Burges, 2010; Ma et al., 2018; Pang et al., 2020), have recently been explored as valuable feedback to enhance earlier stages such as candidate retrieval. One line of work leverages these signals for direct supervision, such as using sampling-bias correction (Yi et al., 2019) or adapting listwise losses (Bruch et al., 2020; Wilm et al., 2023) to better align retrieval models with ranking objectives. Another common approach employs knowledge distillation, where a complex ranker’s knowledge is transferred to a lightweight retrieval model to bridge the performance gap between stages (Tang and Wang, 2018b; Khani et al., 2024). More recently, research has focused on joint or iterative optimization frameworks that seek deeper integration(Sun et al., 2025b). These efforts aim to resolve the systemic inconsistency between stages, either through algorithmic refinements like iterative ensemble sorting (Cao et al., 2025) or reasoning-chain guided generation (Wu et al., 2025), or through architectural designs that explicitly incorporate real-time bids signals (Liu et al., 2025) or optimize inter-stage consistency (Zhao et al., 2023).

A key distinction of our work lies in how ranking signals are structurally utilized. Unlike prior paradigms that primarily treat ranking scores as a downstream supervision signal (Yi et al., 2019; Cao et al., 2025) or a source for distillation (Kang et al., 2023; Khani et al., 2024), POEM repurposes the partial order implied by real-time ranking scores as a structural prior. This order is used to dynamically assemble the model’s input sequence, thereby shifting the role of ranking signals from an optimization target to a generative guide for representation learning.

2.4. Positioning Summary

Table 1 summarizes the key differences between POEM and related paradigms. POEM introduces a novel framework that bridges real-time system intelligence (via ranking signals) and sequential modeling by dynamically constructing sequences based on a preference-derived partial order. This enables a unique form of request-level real-time user interest modeling for the recall stage.

3. Methodology

In this section, we present the POEM framework in detail. We first provide an overview of the entire system and then elaborate on each key component: the dynamic partial-order sequence construction, multi-objective signal fusion, hierarchical learning objective, and the real-time serving architecture.

3.1. Overall Framework

Figure 1. The POEM framework workflow.
Left (Sequence Construction): For a current request, we retrieve the top-ranked items from the user’s previous request (stored in Redis). The Item Feature Construction module calculates a priority score pip_{i} by aggregating multi-dimensional signals (Sc​t​r,Sc​v​rS^{ctr},S^{cvr}, etc.). These items are then partitioned into GG groups based on pip_{i}, from which a Dynamic Partial-Order Sequence is formed via random sampling within each group.
Right(Training Phase): The model uses dual encoders to process the partial-order sequence and user history, generating the representation HuH_{u} via an MLP. The training is optimized by a hierarchical loss combining cross-entropy and pairwise objectives (L​o​s​sC​ELoss_{CE} and L​o​s​sp​a​i​rLoss_{pair}) that considers both user feedback (if​bi_{fb}) and system signals (is​y​si_{sys}).

POEM aims to learn a real-time user interest representation 𝐡u∈ℝd\mathbf{h}_{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} that reflects the system’s current understanding of user preferences based on real-time ranking signals. As illustrated in Figure 1, POEM operates in two phases: training and serving.

During training, for each user request instance, POEM processes two complementary sequences: (1) a historical interaction sequence ShS_{h} constructed from the user’s past interactions, and (2) a dynamic partial-order sequence SrS_{r} constructed from the top-ranked candidate items of the same user’s previous refresh request. These sequences are encoded by independent Transformers, and their fused representations produce 𝐡u\mathbf{h}_{u}, which is optimized via a hierarchical learning objective.

During serving, for each new user request, POEM constructs SrS_{r} from the top-ranked candidate items of the same user’s previous refresh request, processes it alongside ShS_{h}, and generates 𝐡u\mathbf{h}_{u} for real-time ANN-based retrieval. This design ensures temporal causality and leverages the most recent user interest signals.

3.2. Dynamic Partial-Order Sequence Construction

The dynamic partial-order sequence SrS_{r} is the core innovation of POEM, capturing the system’s real-time understanding of user preferences. It is constructed from the top-ranked candidate items of the same user’s previous refresh request, ensuring temporal causality between training and serving. Specifically, let 𝒞={i1,i2,…,iN}\mathcal{C}=\{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{N}\} denote the set of candidate items from the previous refresh request. For each item ii, we have multiple real-time ranking scores from the preceding ranking stage, such as predicted CTR (sctrs^{\text{ctr}}), CVR (scvrs^{\text{cvr}}), and watch time (swts^{\text{wt}}). We denote the vector of ranking scores for item ii as 𝐬i=[sictr,sicvr,siwt]\mathbf{s}_{i}=[s^{\text{ctr}}_{i},s^{\text{cvr}}_{i},s^{\text{wt}}_{i}].

Score Fusion and Ranking.
To establish a consistent partial ordering, we compute a fused score pip_{i} for each item. Direct averaging of per-objective scores is suboptimal due to scale misalignment and varying objective importance. We therefore propose a fusion scheme based on ranking and weighting.

For each objective o∈{ctr,cvr,wt}o\in\{\textrm{ctr},\textrm{cvr},\textrm{wt}\}, we rank all items in the candidate set 𝒞\mathcal{C} by their raw scores sos^{o} in descending order, with rank 1 being the highest. Let ranko⁡(i)\operatorname{rank}^{o}(i) denote the rank of item ii under objective oo. We define a per-objective utility score as the inverse rank:

(1)

so​(i)=1ranko⁡(i).s^{o}(i)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{rank}^{o}(i)}.

Since the candidate set size is fixed, so​(i)s^{o}(i) shares the same numerical range across objectives, making additional normalization unnecessary.

The final fused score pip_{i} is then computed as a weighted combination:

(2)

pi=∑o∈{ctr,cvr,wt}wo⋅so​(i),p_{i}=\sum_{o\in\{\textrm{ctr},\textrm{cvr},\textrm{wt}\}}w^{o}\cdot s^{o}(i),

where wow^{o} is the weight for objective oo and item ii. The candidate set 𝒞\mathcal{C} is sorted in ascending order of pip_{i} to produce the final ranking.

Grouping and Sampling. To construct a sequence that reflects the partial order while maintaining diversity and controlling length, we adopt a grouping and sampling strategy. The sorted list is divided into GG contiguous groups of approximately equal size. From each group, we randomly sample KK items without replacement. The sampled items from group 1 to GG are concatenated to form the sequence S=[i(1,1),…,i(1,K),i(2,1),…,i(G,K)]S=[i_{(1,1)},...,i_{(1,K)},i_{(2,1)},...,i_{(G,K)}]. This approach ensures that the sequence broadly respects the global order (items from higher-score groups appear earlier) while introducing stochasticity within each group. In our implementation, we set G=8G=8 and K=8K=8, resulting in a sequence length of 64.

Sequence Representation. Each item ii in SS is represented as a quintuple:

(3)

𝐫i=(rankctr​(i),rankcvr​(i),rankwt​(i),pi,ID​(i)),\mathbf{r}_{i}=\left(\text{rank}^{\text{ctr}}(i),\text{rank}^{\text{cvr}}(i),\text{rank}^{\text{wt}}(i),p_{i},\text{ID}(i)\right),

where the ID​(i)\text{ID}(i) is the item’s unique identifier. To obtain a dense embedding 𝐞i∈ℝd\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}, the quintuple is projected through a hybrid encoding layer:

𝐞i=𝐖feat⋅[rankctr⁡(i),rankcvr⁡(i),rankwt⁡(i),pi]⊤+𝐄embed​(ID⁡(i)),\mathbf{e}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{\text{feat}}\cdot\left[\operatorname{rank}^{\text{ctr}}(i),\;\operatorname{rank}^{\text{cvr}}(i),\;\operatorname{rank}^{\text{wt}}(i),\;p_{i}\right]^{\top}+\mathbf{E}_{\text{embed}}\bigl(\operatorname{ID}(i)\bigr),

where 𝐖feat∈ℝd×4\mathbf{W}_{\text{feat}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times 4} is a learnable weight matrix for the continuous features, and 𝐄embed\mathbf{E}_{\text{embed}} denotes the item embedding table. The sequence of all embedded items 𝐒𝐫=[𝐞1,𝐞2,…,𝐞L]\mathbf{S_{r}}=[\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{L}] (with L=G×K=64L=G\times K=64) forms the input to the subsequent transformer encoder.

3.3. User Representation Learning via Dual Sequences

POEM processes two complementary sequences to learn a comprehensive user representation:

Historical Interaction Sequence (ShS_{h}). We construct a fixed-length sequence of the user’s recent interactions for modeling long-term preferences. Each interaction is represented by concatenating multiple feature embeddings including item ID, content tags, and auxiliary signals.

Dual-Sequence Encoding. We employ two independent Transformer encoders (Vaswani et al., 2017) to process the two sequences in parallel. Let Encoderh\text{Encoder}_{h} denote the encoder for the historical interaction sequence ShS_{h}, and Encoderr\text{Encoder}_{r} denote the encoder for the dynamic partial-order sequence SrS_{r}. Both encoders follow the standard Transformer architecture with multiple layers of multi-head self-attention and position-wise feed-forward networks, sharing the same architectural configuration but not parameters.

For each encoder, we append a special token to the end of its input sequence to aggregate sequence-level information. Let 𝐡h∈ℝd\mathbf{h}_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and 𝐡r∈ℝd\mathbf{h}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} denote the output vectors corresponding to these special tokens from the two encoders, respectively. We then fuse these two representations through concatenation followed by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to obtain the final user representation:

(4)

𝐡u=MLP​([𝐡h;𝐡r]),\mathbf{h}_{u}=\text{MLP}\left([\mathbf{h}_{h};\mathbf{h}_{r}]\right),

where [⋅;⋅][\cdot;\cdot] denotes vector concatenation, and the MLP consists of two linear layers with a non-linear activation in between.

3.4. Hierarchical Learning Objective

We design a learning objective that aligns the user representation 𝐡u\mathbf{h}_{u} with items that reflect both system preference and genuine user satisfaction.

Positive Samples. For each training instance, we define two types of positive items to bridge the gap between user behavior and system intelligence:

•

User-Feedback Positive (ifb+i^{+}_{\text{fb}}): An item from 𝒞\mathcal{C} that was actually exposed to the user and received positive feedback. This grounds the learning in actual user behavior.

•

System-Preferred Positive (isys+i^{+}_{\text{sys}}): The item with the highest multi-objective score pip_{i} among the candidate set 𝒞\mathcal{C}. This represents the item the system believes the user is most likely to engage with positively.

While ifb+i^{+}_{\text{fb}} represents the ultimate goal, it is often sparse and subject to selection bias. In contrast, isys+i^{+}_{\text{sys}} serves as a distillation signal from the sophisticated ranking stage. By incorporating isys+i^{+}_{\text{sys}}, POEM ensures that the retrieval model is cascade-aware, generating candidates that are more likely to be prioritized by downstream rankers, thereby improving the overall delivery rate in the production pipeline.
The final positive set for the instance is 𝒫={isys+,ifb+}\mathcal{P}=\{i^{+}_{\text{sys}},i^{+}_{\text{fb}}\}.

Hard Negative Mining via Dynamic Graph. In-batch random negative sampling often yields items that are too easy to distinguish. To enhance discriminability, we mine hard negatives from a global item-item similarity graph 𝒢\mathcal{G} constructed offline using the Swing algorithm (Yang et al., 2020), which captures co-occurrence patterns. Specifically, for each positive item i+∈𝒫={if​b+,is​y​s+}i^{+}\in\mathcal{P}=\{i^{+}_{fb},i^{+}_{sys}\}, we independently retrieve its top-MM most similar neighbors in 𝒢\mathcal{G}, denoted as 𝒩⁡(i+,M)\mathcal{N}(i^{+},M). This process results in two positive-conditioned item-to-item negative pairs: the user-feedback negative if​b−i^{-}_{fb} mined around if​b+i^{+}_{fb}, and the system-preferred negative is​y​s−i^{-}_{sys} mined around is​y​s+i^{+}_{sys}, respectively, ensuring consistency with the hierarchical training structure illustrated in Figure 1. To balance hardness and diversity, we adopt a mixed strategy: with probability ρ\rho, we select the item with the third-lowest similarity score within 𝒩⁡(i+,M)\mathcal{N}(i^{+},M) as the hard negative ihard−i^{-}_{\text{hard}}; otherwise, we randomly sample an item from 𝒩⁡(i+,M)\mathcal{N}(i^{+},M). This strategy, informed by empirical analysis, provides negatives that are semantically related but likely not preferred in the current context. The mining is performed dynamically for each training instance, ensuring that both (if​b+,if​b−)(i^{+}_{fb},i^{-}_{fb}) and (is​y​s+,is​y​s−)(i^{+}_{sys},i^{-}_{sys}) pairs remain aligned with the specific partial-order context.

Loss Function. We combine a standard cross-entropy loss with a margin-based pairwise loss to form the hierarchical objective.

(5)

ℒ=ℒCE+λ​ℒpair,\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{pair}},

where λ\lambda is a balancing hyperparameter.

The cross-entropy loss ℒCE\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} uses in-batch negatives:

(6)

ℒCE=−1B∑b=1Blogexp⁡(sim​(𝐡ub,𝐞ib+)/τ)∑j∈𝒩bexp⁡(sim​(𝐡ub,𝐞j)/τ),\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\log\frac{\exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_{u}^{b},\mathbf{e}_{i_{b}^{+}})/\tau)}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{b}}\exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_{u}^{b},\mathbf{e}_{j})/\tau)},

where 𝐡ub\mathbf{h}_{u}^{b} is the user representation for the bb-th instance, ib+i_{b}^{+} is a positive item sampled from 𝒫b\mathcal{P}_{b}, 𝒩b\mathcal{N}_{b} is the set of negative items (in-batch negatives) for this instance, sim​(⋅,⋅)\text{sim}(\cdot,\cdot) denotes cosine similarity, and τ\tau is a temperature parameter. Additionally, we incorporate a sampling bias correction mechanism (Yi et al., 2019) to mitigate popularity bias in training data.

The pairwise loss ℒpair\mathcal{L}_{\text{pair}} explicitly enforces a margin between the positive and hard negative similarities:

(7)

ℒpair=1B​∑b=1Bmax⁡(0,γ+sim​(𝐡ub,𝐞ihard,b−)−sim​(𝐡ub,𝐞ib+)),\mathcal{L}_{\text{pair}}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\max\left(0,\gamma+\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_{u}^{b},\mathbf{e}_{i^{-}_{\text{hard},b}})-\text{sim}(\mathbf{h}_{u}^{b},\mathbf{e}_{i_{b}^{+}})\right),

where γ>0\gamma>0 is the margin. This loss directly optimizes the model to distinguish between the positive item and a challenging alternative.

3.5. Real-Time Serving Architecture

To deploy POEM online, we design a low-latency serving pipeline that strictly adheres to the temporal causality principle: the user representation for the current request is derived from ranking signals of the previous request. The real-time process for each user request is as follows:

(1)

Input Signal Preparation: For the incoming request, the system fetches two key inputs: (a) the user’s historical interaction sequence ShS_{h}; and (b) the list of top-ranked items and their multi-objective ranking scores {𝐬i}\{\mathbf{s}_{i}\} from the user’s immediately preceding request, which are retrieved from a low-latency cache (e.g., Redis).

(2)

Sequence Encoding & Inference: The dynamic sequence SrS_{r} is constructed from previous request signals (Section 3.2) and encoded together with ShS_{h} by the POEM model to produce 𝐡u\mathbf{h}_{u}. This step is deeply optimized for millisecond-level latency.

(3)

ANN Retrieval: 𝐡u\mathbf{h}_{u} is used as a query to retrieve the final candidate set from a pre-built Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) index over the entire item corpus.

(4)

Feedback Loop for Subsequent Requests: Concurrently, the candidate items for the current request proceed through downstream ranking stages (e.g., coarse and fine ranking). The final top-ranked items from all recall sources along with their scores are cached. This cached data then serves as the input for constructing SrS_{r} when the same user makes the next refresh request, thereby closing the real-time adaptation loop.

This serving architecture ensures that every user request is served with an embedding that incorporates the system’s most recent understanding of user preference, which is precisely one request cycle fresh. It fulfills the design goal of request-level real-time interest modeling while maintaining the low latency required for large-scale production.

4. Experiments

We conduct comprehensive offline and online experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed POEM framework. The experiments are designed to answer the following research questions:

•

RQ1: Does POEM outperform state-of-the-art sequential recommendation baselines, both offline and online?

•

RQ2: How does each component of POEM (dynamic sequence construction, multi-signal fusion, and hierarchical sample strategy) contribute to its performance?

•

RQ3: Is POEM effective in improving recommendation diversity and real-time responsiveness?

4.1. Experimental Setup

4.1.1. Dataset

We use a large-scale industrial dataset collected from a global-scale short-video recommendation platform serving over 400 million daily active users with more than 50 billion daily interactions.
To ensure a robust offline evaluation, we construct a large-scale benchmark from six consecutive days of production logs.We split the data chronologically: the first five days for training, and the first hour of the sixth day for testing, to better match the real-time characteristics of industrial recommendation systems

Each training/testing instance corresponds to a user request. The candidate item set (approximately 1000 items) for this request is constructed from the top-ranked results of the user’s previous request, with the user’s true feedback (e.g., clicks, long-play) on these items serving as the label.

4.1.2. Baseline Models

We compare POEM with the following representative baseline models:

•

DSSM (Huang et al., 2013): A classic non-sequential dual-tower model that learns static semantic embeddings. It provides a baseline to contrast static matching with POEM’s use of dynamic ranking signals.

•

SASRec (Kang and McAuley, 2018): A unidirectional Transformer-based sequential model with self-attention.

•

CAIN (variant) (Guo et al., 2025): Originally a ranking model leveraging Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) for local contextual modeling, we adapt it for the retrieval setting by retaining only its TCN-based user encoder. This serves as a strong context-aware baseline, contrasting its local sequence modeling with POEM’s partial-order-driven representation learning.

4.1.3. Evaluation Metrics

We adopt both offline and online evaluation metrics.
Offline metrics computed on the test set include:

•

Hit Rate (HR@K): Proportion of test positive items appearing in the top-K recommendation list.

•

Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@K): Ranking quality metric.

4.1.4. Implementation Details

All models are implemented in Tensorflow and trained in identical hardware/software environments. We use the AdamW optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001 and cosine annealing scheduler. The batch size is set to 1024. For POEM, the Transformer encoder has 4 layers, hidden dimension 128, and 1 attention heads. The sequence construction parameters are: number of groups G=8G=8, samples per group K=8K=8, sequence length 64. The loss balancing weight λ=0.1\lambda=0.1 and margin γ=0.2\gamma=0.2.

4.2. Main Results(RQ1&RQ3)

4.2.1. Offline Results

Table 2 presents the offline performance comparison on a 1-hour test set collected immediately after the training period. POEM significantly outperforms all baseline models across all metrics. Specifically, compared to the strongest sequential baseline CAIN Variant, POEM achieves a relative improvement of 15.9% in HR@50 and 29.9% in NDCG@50.

4.2.2. Analysis: Temporal Generalization of POEM

To investigate POEM’s real-time adaptability, we contrast its performance on the 1-hour and 1-day test sets (Table 2). While POEM maintains a consistent lead over all baselines in both settings, its relative advantage is markedly more pronounced on the 1-hour set. This stems from the nature of SrS_{r}: the partial-order sequence is constructed from top-ranked candidates of the preceding request, making it highly sensitive to real-time shifts in user interest, content pool, and ranking policy. Training and evaluation on a short, contiguous window allows POEM to closely track the current system state, whereas day-level evaluation spans a longer horizon over which the underlying recommendation logic and user distribution may shift, diluting signal precision.

To further quantify this temporal sensitivity, we partition test requests by time gap from the training cutoff and report HR@50 for POEM and the strongest sequential baseline (CAIN Variant) in Table 4.

Analysis of Positive Sampling Strategies

As illustrated in Table 7, the Dual-Pos strategy (POEM) significantly outperforms single-sampling variants in both offline alignment and online utility. We observe that neither user feedback nor system preference alone is sufficient:

•

Limitations of Only-FB: While ifb+i^{+}_{\text{fb}} represents genuine user satisfaction, it suffers from severe Selection Bias and sparsity. Since ifb+i^{+}_{\text{fb}} is derived only from items already exposed and clicked, training solely on it forces the retrieval model to overfit the historical exposure policy. This leads to Objective Misalignment: retrieval may capture items with high click probability but low multi-objective utility (e.g., short watch time), which are subsequently filtered out by downstream rankers. This is evidenced by the lowest Delivery Rate (1.052%), indicating poor alignment with the overall system goal.

•

Role of Only-Sys: In contrast, incorporating isys+i^{+}_{\text{sys}} provides Cascade Awareness. By learning from the system’s top-ranked items, the model "distills" the multi-objective knowledge of the ranking stage. Although this slightly shifts the focus from raw clicks, it significantly improves Delivery Efficiency. As shown, Only-Sys already achieves a higher Delivery Rate (1.472%) and Online Gain than Only-FB, proving that aligning with the ranker is crucial for the survival of candidates in the pipeline.

Impact of Hard Negative Mining.

Next, we evaluate the contribution of dynamic graph-based hard negative mining. As shown in Table 7, replacing our hard negatives with simple random negatives (w/o Hard-Neg) leads to a substantial drop in HR@50 (from 0.6661 to 0.6154) and NDCG@50. This confirms that in global-scale systems, random negatives are often too "easy" to provide sufficient gradient information. By mining semantically related but non-interacted items via the Swing graph, POEM learns a more precise decision boundary, effectively distinguishing subtle user preferences in a crowded candidate space.

4.4. Case Studies

To quantify POEM’s real-time responsiveness, we visualize the constructed partial-order sequences for a typical user across two consecutive refresh requests. As shown in Figure 2, although no new user clicks occurred during this interval, the input partial-order sequence SrS_{r} exhibits a sequence diff ratio of 96.8%.
This significant change is driven by the interplay between the evolving candidate pool and our adaptive sampling strategy, which captures the latest ranking signals from the preceding stage.

This shift in SrS_{r} effectively "steers" the user embedding 𝐡u\mathbf{h}_{u} in the vector space, leading to an 86.2% difference in the final retrieval results. As visualized by the item screenshots, the system captures a subtle transition from "Comedy" to "Film & TV" based on the latest candidate availability. This demonstrates that POEM can adapt to the system’s latest understanding of the user even during "passive" periods, whereas static sequence models (e.g., SASRec) would yield identical results.

5. Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced POEM (Partial-Order Enhanced Modeling), a novel real-time recommendation framework that directly integrates cascade intelligence into sequential user modeling. To bridge the gap between static historical sequences and dynamic system context, POEM constructs a partial-order sequence from the multi-objective ranking signals of the user’s preceding request, enabling request-level re-evaluation of user interest. POEM achieves this through a dual-sequence architecture, a group-random sampling strategy for partial-order construction, and a hierarchical objective with hard negative mining.

Extensive evaluations on large-scale Kuaishou data validate POEM’s effectiveness. Offline, it outperforms SASRec and CAIN in accuracy. More importantly, online A/B tests across two major scenarios confirm its practical impact, with significant gains in user engagement (+0.249% and +0.213% in average usage time per user). These results substantiate that leveraging ranking signals as a structural prior is key to real-time, cascade-aware recommendation. Future work includes improving robustness to noisy ranking signals and extending the partial-order paradigm to cross-domain settings, where system intelligence can guide broader exploration.
