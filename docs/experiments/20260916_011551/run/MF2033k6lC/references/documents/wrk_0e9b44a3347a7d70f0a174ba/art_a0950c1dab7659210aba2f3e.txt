Generative deep learning models have demonstrated strong performance in data generation tasks; graph-based variants extend this capability by exploiting the relational structure of the data, which is explicitly encoded as a graph. However, graph deep learning approaches, such as variational graph autoencoders (VGAEs), often suffer from oversmoothing, which degrades the quality of generated representations. While oversmoothing has been studied in large-scale graph settings, its impact under low sensor (i.e., sparsely connected graph) density remains largely unexplored. In this work, we propose the Mean-Field Variational Graph Autoencoder (MFVGAE) which integrates a smoothing operation based on mean-field layers with VGAEs. This refinement improves embedding quality by regularizing latent representations, enhancing local discriminative power in graph-based VAEs while constraining oversmoothing. We evaluate MFVGAE on a data generation task, posed as a matrix completion problem, using real-world electromagnetic and air quality exposure measurements collected in Belgium. Results show that MFVGAE outperforms other statistical and deep learning methods, including the standard VGAE, under two sparse and irregular real-world data conditions.
