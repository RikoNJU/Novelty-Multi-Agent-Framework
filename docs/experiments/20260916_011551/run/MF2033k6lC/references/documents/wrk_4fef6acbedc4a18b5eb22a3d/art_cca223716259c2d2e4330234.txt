In the field of Remote Sensing Image-Text Retrieval (RSITR), introducing auxiliary tasks like position reconstruction is an effective strategy to boost fine-grained cross-modal alignment. However, existing multi-task learning methods mostly use fixed weights to jointly optimize the primary retrieval task and auxiliary reconstruction tasks, implicitly assuming stable supervision quality of each task throughout the training process. In remote sensing scenarios, auxiliary reconstruction tasks suffer from severe uncertainty and inherent noise caused by cloud occlusion, illumination variation, and sensor noise. Fitting these unreliable supervision signals with fixed weights will interfere with shared representation learning, damage the optimization stability of the primary task, and impair the model’s generalization and robustness. To address this issue, we propose B-PRCLIP, a Bayesian uncertainty-aware multi-task learning framework for RSITR. It reformulates multi-task joint optimization as a maximum likelihood estimation problem under the homoscedastic uncertainty assumption. By introducing learnable log-variance parameters for auxiliary tasks, it adaptively evaluates their supervision credibility and optimization difficulty, and dynamically adjusts their weights in the total loss. This method automatically mitigates the negative impact of unreliable reconstruction signals on the shared encoder, improves multi-task collaborative optimization stability, and guides the model to focus on robust cross-modal semantic alignment. Experiments on two public benchmarks RSICD and RSITMD show that our method maintains excellent retrieval accuracy on clean test sets, significantly improves training stability, and exhibits stronger anti-interference ability under noise perturbations, validating the effectiveness of the proposed uncertainty-aware weighting mechanism.
