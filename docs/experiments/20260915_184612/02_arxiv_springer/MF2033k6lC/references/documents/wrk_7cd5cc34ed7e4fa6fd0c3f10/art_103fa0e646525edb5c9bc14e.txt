Abstract

Abstract In the contemporary era of digital transformation, large enterprises are confronted with three significant challenges: the presence of isolated human resource data, the necessity of privacy compliance, and the imperative for dynamic decision-making. Conventional static models are ill-equipped to capture the evolution of skills and organisational changes, while regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) impede data circulation. In order to address the aforementioned issues, we hereby introduce Fed Hyper KG-HR, a novel framework that pioneers the integration of hypergraph neural networks and federated learning. This innovative development solves, for the first time, these core HR challenges. A significant innovation is the four-dimensional spatiotemporal ontology model, which has been shown to capture complex cross-organizational relationships with greater accuracy, achieving 18.3% higher collaborative identification accuracy than traditional federated learning methods. The innovative temporal reasoning mechanism underpinning our dynamic decision-making process represents a significant advancement in the field of skill modelling. This breakthrough has been demonstrated to achieve a promotion prediction accuracy that exceeds 93%, while concurrently reducing false positive rates in resignation warnings to below 3%. This represents a substantial enhancement, with a 50% improvement over conventional industrial systems. In the field of privacy protection, our innovative adaptive perturbation technique has been demonstrated to achieve an accuracy of 89% in decision-making, while maintaining a zero data leakage (Adaptive perturbation technology achieves 89% decision accuracy at ε=1.2, with controllable privacy leakage risk) rate. This enhancement in efficiency, as measured by the accuracy-to-time ratio, has been shown to exceed 35% when compared to conventional baseline methods. Empirical evidence has demonstrated a growth in cross-departmental resource matching efficiency that exceeds 50%. This establishes a ‘knowledge-driven’ paradigm, which is unique, and exhibits excellent transferability across industries, such as finance.

Introduction Driven by the wave of digital, human resource management (HRM) in enterprises is undergoing a profound transformation from being experience-driven to data-driven [ 1 ]. However, due to their complex organizational structures and multi-level management systems, large central enterprises generally face challenges such as isolated HR data, weak privacy protection, and difficulties in dynamic knowledge modeling [ 2 ]. Taking a certain central enterprise group as an example, its 12 subsidiaries have a HR data volume of 10 TB, but the accuracy rate of cross-departmental collaboration identification is less than 65%, and the efficiency of employee turnover prediction is low [ 3 ] Traditional information systems, with static data models and one-way decision-making logic, struggle to meet the time-series requirements such as the dynamic evolution of employee skills and frequent adjustments to organizational structures [ 4 ]– [ 5 ]. At the same time, the strengthening of global privacy protection regulations, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and China’s Personal Information Protection Law, further restricts the free flow of data across organizations [ 6 ]. Against this backdrop, how to build an HR knowledge system that can achieve privacy compliance and support dynamic decision-making has become an urgent scientific problem to be solved [ 7 , 8 – 9 ]. Addressing the aforementioned gaps, this study proposes the “Federated hyper graph-Dynamic Knowledge Graph” fusion framework (Fed Hyper KG-HR), aiming to achieve three major innovative goals: Firstly, designing a federated learning architecture based on hyper graph, which utilizes differential privacy protection and potential hyper edge generation techniques to enhance the collaborative recognition accuracy of HR data from multiple subsidiaries of central enterprises to 85% [ 10 ]; secondly, constructing a four-dimensional spatio temporal ontology model (enterprise-department-position-employee + timestamp), combined with a Temporal Graph Database, to dynamically track the evolution path of employee skills [ 11 , 12 ]; thirdly, developing an interpretable decision engine that compresses the response time for resignation alerts to 50 milliseconds and supports transparent deduction for promotion decisions [ 13 ]. The empirical demonstration of this framework in Group G shows that its talent turnover prediction F1 score reaches 0.89, an improvement of 24% compared to traditional models [ 14 ], validating the effectiveness of the technical solution. Research progress and theoretical gaps Technical evolution and limitations of federated hyper graph neural networks The combination of federated learning and graph neural networks has emerged as a key technology for breaking through data silos in recent years [ 19 ]. The foundational Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm (McMahan et al., 2017) was explicitly designed to address the challenge of **non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) data across clients, which is a defining characteristic of the federated learning setting [ 20 ]. As noted in the original work, their approach was demonstrated to be robust to unbalanced and non-IID data distributions. While FedAvg provides a strong baseline, its performance can degrade under extreme degrees of data heterogeneity, which has motivated a large body of subsequent research, including methods based on graph neural networks, to further improve robustness and model convergence in these challenging non-IID environments. For instance, in multinational central enterprises, there are significant differences in the skill distribution of employees across different subsidiaries (e.g., subsidiary A focuses on technology research and development, while subsidiary B focuses on marketing). In such scenarios, the collaborative recognition accuracy of traditional federated models is less than 60% [ 21 ]. To address this challenge, the HyperGCN (hyper graph Convolutional Neural Network) was introduced, whose hyper edge structure can model complex relationships among multiple entities [ 22 , 23 – 24 ]. Traditional federated graph neural networks (e.g., FedGCN, FedGNN) primarily rely on simple graph structures such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) or Graph Attention Networks (GATs), which are limited in modeling complex relationships involving multiple entities. In contrast, our Fed Hyper KG-HR framework introduces hypergraph neural networks, which can capture higher-order relationships among multiple entities, making it particularly useful in complex organizational settings where relationships are not limited to pairwise interactions. Most existing federated graph models assume static graph structures, which cannot effectively capture the dynamic evolution of relationships over time. Traditional federated learning methods often use basic differential privacy techniques, which may not be sufficient for highly sensitive data in enterprise settings. Our framework employs an adaptive perturbation technique that achieves a high level of privacy protection (zero data leakage) while maintaining high decision accuracy (89%), a significant improvement over conventional methods. Existing federated graph models often struggle with scalability, especially when dealing with large-scale heterogeneous data from multiple subsidiaries. Our framework is designed to handle large-scale data efficiently, with a distributed training architecture that ensures the computational complexity grows sub-linearly with the data size, making it suitable for multinational corporations. Many federated graph models lack interpretability, making it difficult to explain the decision-making process. We introduce an interpretable decision engine that generates transparent rules for tasks such as promotion prediction and resignation warning, enhancing trust and transparency in the decision-making process. This selective updating reduces the amount of data that needs to be communicated between subsidiaries and the central server, saving time and resources. Additionally, by using a sparse mask, it ensures that only non-sensitive information is shared, enhancing privacy protection.
