Existing multivariate time series anomaly detection methods are prone to interference from redundant channel noise in complex high-dimensional environments, leading to failure in spatial topology perception. To address this, we propose DCF-GSL, a spatio-temporal anomaly detection model based on dynamic channel fusion and graph structure learning. The model first captures multi-resolution temporal dependencies via multi-scale temporal convolutions. It then constructs a time-adaptive graph structure through learnable node embeddings and Top-K selection, mining local spatial correlations and global topological features by combining multi-head attention with topological persistent homology. A self-supervised masked attention mechanism with dynamic contrastive loss drives the model to actively learn complementary inter-channel relationships in latent space, adaptively filtering redundant noise and reinforcing key feature representations. Finally, a decoder predicts sensor values to generate normalized anomaly scores, and an adaptive thresholding strategy based on maximizing the F1-score enables precise detection. Experiments on SWaT, MSL, and SMD benchmarks demonstrate that DCF-GSL outperforms OmniAnomaly, GDN, TranAD, and other baselines in terms of F1-score.
