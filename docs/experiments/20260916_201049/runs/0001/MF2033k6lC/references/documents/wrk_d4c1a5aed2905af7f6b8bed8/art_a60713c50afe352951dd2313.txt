The rise of large language models (LLMs) has transformed traffic flow prediction in intelligent transportation systems. Unlike traditional methods dependent on structured inputs and handcrafted features, LLMs offer superior semantic reasoning and context modeling, enabling flexible processing of heterogeneous traffic data. This review examines LLM applications in traffic flow prediction, focusing on two paradigms: structure-guided modeling and language-driven generative prediction. We analyze representative models—including TPLLM, ST-LLM, UrbanGPT, and TF-LLM—highlighting their architectures, alignment strategies, and generalization capabilities. The survey also addresses current challenges in structural alignment, zero-shot forecasting, and real-world deployment, providing insights for developing next-generation predictive systems with enhanced interpretability and adaptability.
