Purpose: Over 5M+ people in India rely on Indian Sign Language for communication with specially-abled people, and yet technological support remains very limited. We developed a real-time ISL recognition system as a proof-of-concept demonstrating CPU-based deployment feasibility. Methods: We adapted and optimized existing HWGAT architecture with 9.7M parameters (52.7% reduction from baseline) processing 29 MediaPipe-extracted keypoints enhanced with Fourier features, cyclic shifts, and learnable positional encodings. Our heuristic motion-gated mechanism uses simple displacement thresholds for continuous sign detection. Results: On a limited 6-class dataset (76 samples), the system achieved 78.57% validation accuracy. The 95% CI (49.2%-95.3%) reflects higher variance due to a small sample size, which indicates that the results should be interpreted as preliminary. Ablation studies show that incremental improvements are as such: learnable encodings (+8.33%), Fourier features (+7.14%), and cyclic shifts (+7.14%). Conclusion: Our work demonstrates the feasibility of a CPU-based ISL recognition but acknowledges its significant limitations: a small vocabulary, limited amount of training data and simpler motion-gating heuristics. Findings established baseline for future large-scale studies.
