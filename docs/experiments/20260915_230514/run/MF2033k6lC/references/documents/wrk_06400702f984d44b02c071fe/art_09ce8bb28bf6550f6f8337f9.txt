With the rapid development of IoT technologies and widespread deployment of intelligent sensing devices, dynamic sensor selection has become crucial for balancing recognition accuracy and energy efficiency in applications like health monitoring. Existing runtime assessment methods suffer from limited predictive utility for future sensor quality and fail to capture dynamic spatio-temporal dependencies among interconnected devices. To this end, we propose a utility-aware sensor selection framework that integrates adaptive spatio-temporal forecasting with attention-based quality assessment. Our approach employs a Dynamic Graph Learning-based Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (DGL-STGCN) to model evolving device correlations and predict sensor states, while an attention mechanism assesses future sensor utility through learned importance scores. This enables proactive sensor selection optimized for both recognition accuracy and energy consumption, explicitly accounting for switching overheads. We develop and evaluate a prototype system designed for wearable devices to monitor locomotion activities. Results show that our system achieves a 14% accuracy improvement over single-sensor baselines, a 25% reduction in energy consumption compared to full-sensor deployment, and a 2% higher accuracy than state-of-the-art methods at equivalent energy levels.
