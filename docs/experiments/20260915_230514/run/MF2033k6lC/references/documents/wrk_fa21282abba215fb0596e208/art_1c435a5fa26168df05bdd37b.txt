Large graphs can be considered to be made of small subgraphs, called graphlets , that make up its fundamental building blocks. Therefore, counting graphlet frequencies is valuable for understanding structural characteristics and function of a given graph. In this paper, we propose an efficient algorithm CaTSCAN for counting the exact frequencies of typed graphlets of size up to 5 nodes in heterogeneous information networks, where the nodes of the graph (and graphlets) are assigned different types. Existing work has only addressed simpler problems, either focused on counting untyped graphlets of sizes up to 5 nodes, or in the case of typed graphlets, only considered counting graphlets with 3 and 4 nodes. CaTSCAN avoids excessive enumeration of individual graphlet instances by deriving the counts of larger graphlets from smaller ones with non-trivial and highly efficient combinatorics, while preserving type information. In an experimental evaluation, we show that CaTSCAN outperforms existing typed graphlet solutions on real-world datasets by up to 4 orders of magnitude for counting 3- and 4-node graphlets, and counts 5-node graphlets in less time than existing solutions count 3- and 4-node graphlets. CaTSCAN can even out-speed some untyped baselines on larger graphs.
