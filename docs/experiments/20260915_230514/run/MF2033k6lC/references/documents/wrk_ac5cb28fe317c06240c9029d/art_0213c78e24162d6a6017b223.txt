Over the past few decades, there has been a substantial rise in date production in Tunisia. However, this growth in production has been marred by a notable increase in losses incurred during harvesting, storage, marketing, and technological processing. The significant quantities of by-products resulting from these losses remain largely unused by the population due to their poor quality. Biotechnological processing of lower-quality dates with a hard texture into derived products may give them a presentation, a structure and functionality. The aim of this work is to enhance the value of date fruit of inferior grades by using it in syrup production made according to different processing conditions. Four processes for date syrup preparation were used: conventional, by enzymatic extraction, microwave and intermittent processes (conventional—microwave) were investigated. The corresponding syrups were characterized and compared. The overall phenolic (TPC) and flavonoid (TFC) concentrations, along with their associated antioxidant capabilities were performed for each sample of the date syrup. Results showed effective outcomes of the used process for date syrup preparation on their functional properties. Date syrup prepared according to the combined process gave the highest phenolic and flavonoid contents (504.127 mg Gallic Acid Equivalent/100 g and 190.892 mg Catechin Equivalent/100 g of syrup respectively). The results of our study hold significant relevance for both researchers in the field and the fruit-processing industry. They offer opportunities to create novel food products with elevated antioxidant properties, aligning with the preferences of a contemporary consumer demographic.
