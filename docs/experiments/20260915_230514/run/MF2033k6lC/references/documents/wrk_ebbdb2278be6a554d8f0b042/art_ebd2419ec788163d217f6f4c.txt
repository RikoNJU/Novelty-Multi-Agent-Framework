Abstract

Abstract Abstractive text summarization is a critical yet challenging task in natural language processing, requiring models to generate concise, coherent, and factually accurate summaries. Existing transformer-based architectures, such as BART and T5, often underperform in scenarios where language semantics evolve over time or when lexical diversity is limited. These limitations result in summaries that lack contextual relevance and semantic generalization, especially in dynamic domains such as news reporting. In this work, we present a lightweight hybrid abstractive summarization model that enhances temporal awareness and linguistic flexibility. The model integrates synonym-based augmentation and time-sensitive embeddings into a base transformer encoder-decoder architecture, enabling the generation of temporally aligned and semantically enriched summaries. To further improve reliability, we introduce a factual alignment evaluation module that assesses entity-level consistency using named entity recognition. We conduct extensive experiments on two benchmark datasets, XSum and CNN/Daily Mail, and evaluate the model using a wide range of metrics, including ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR, BERTScore, and factual consistency checks. The ablation study confirms the individual contributions of temporal modeling and synonym enrichment, with the combined model achieving up to 8.3% ROUGE-1 and 7.9% METEOR improvements over the baseline. The model demonstrates practical applicability for summarization systems requiring both temporal relevance and semantic robustness, while maintaining CPU efficiency and interpretability. Implications for downstream tasks like news trend analysis are also explored.

Body

Introduction Automatic text summarization has become a fundamental task in natural language processing (NLP), particularly in the context of exponential growth in digital information. From social media feeds to scholarly publications, the ability to automatically condense lengthy texts into concise and coherent summaries is essential for improving comprehension, reducing cognitive overload, and enhancing decision-making efficiency. Among the two dominant approaches—extractive and abstractive—abstractive summarization is more challenging but also more powerful, as it generates novel sentences rather than merely selecting fragments from the source [ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] [ 4 ]. Recent progress in transformer-based models, such as BART [ 5 ], PEGASUS [ 1 ], PRIMERA [ 6 ], T5 [ 7 ] and GPT-4 [ 8 ], has led to substantial improvements in abstractive summarization. These models leverage encoder-decoder architectures and large-scale pretraining to capture contextualized semantic information and produce fluent, human-like summaries. Nevertheless, despite these advances, several challenges remain. First, existing models often lack temporal awareness , which is critical in domains like news, where the context and relevance of events evolve over time [ 9 ] [ 10 ]. Language usage, topical salience, and even entity significance may drift over time, and ignoring this drift can lead to outdated or semantically irrelevant summaries [ 11 ] [ 12 ]. Second, many models exhibit lexical rigidity , relying on narrow vocabularies and repetitive phrases [ 13 ]. This reduces their ability to generalize semantically and impacts fluency and diversity, especially when encountering rare or unseen lexical patterns. Third, ensuring factual consistency between the generated summary and the source remains an open problem [ 14 ]. Models may “hallucinate” content not grounded in the input, leading to plausible-sounding but inaccurate outputs [ 15 ] [ 16 ]. To address these challenges, this paper presents an incrementally enhanced abstractive summarization model based on the BART architecture (i.e., transformer-based architecture). We incorporate three lightweight modules designed to improve semantic adaptability, temporal alignment, and factual reliability: (1) synonym-based augmentation using WordNet [ 17 ] to increase lexical diversity in training, (2) temporal-aware input tagging that injects publication-year context into the encoder, and (3) a post-evaluation factual alignment module that compares entity-level consistency using named entity recognition (NER) [ 13 ] and confusion matrices. Rather than introducing new architectural layers, our approach retains model simplicity and maintains computational efficiency for real-world applicability. We evaluate the proposed model on two benchmark datasets (i.e., XSum [ 15 ] and CNN/Daily Mail [ 16 ]) using a suite of metrics including ROUGE, METEOR, BLEU, and BERTScore [ 18 ] [ 19 ], along with NER-based factual alignment checks. The empirical results show statistically significant improvements over the baseline BART model, particularly in METEOR and ROUGE-1 scores. Furthermore, we demonstrate the practical value of the proposed abstractive summarization model in downstream tasks such as news trend analysis and categorization, where temporal context and entity consistency are essential. By integrating temporal signals and synonym diversity through simple input-level enhancements, and applying factuality evaluation through interpretable diagnostics, the proposed model advances the reliability and adaptability of summarization models while remaining accessible and efficient. The rest of the paper, however, is organized as follows. Section 2 presents a related work to the study, and Sects. 3 describe preliminaries, technical details, and our proposed summarization model. Section 4 shows empirical results, and Sect. 5 concludes the paper. Related work Automatic document summarization has evolved significantly with the advent of neural language models, especially in the domain of abstractive summarization, which aims to generate novel, semantically coherent sentences rather than merely extracting text spans. The initial breakthroughs were driven by sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) frameworks enhanced with attention mechanisms, enabling neural models to dynamically focus on relevant parts of the input sequence during generation [ 20 ] [ 21 ]. These mechanisms helped mitigate the limitations of fixed-size context windows and improved relevance in generated summaries. Subsequently, the introduction of the transformer architecture by Vaswani et al. (2019) [ 22 ] marked a paradigm shift by eliminating recurrence and enabling self-attention mechanisms that model long-range dependencies more effectively. This innovation improved scalability and parallelism, making it the architectural backbone of virtually all modern summarization models, including BART [ 5 ], PEGASUS [ 1 ], T5 [ 7 ], and others. The transformer’s encoder-decoder structure has become a standard for generative tasks, including summarization, due to its ability to represent complex semantics across large text corpora. Transformer-based summarization models Transformer-based pretrained language models such as BERT [ 23 ], GPT [ 24 ], BART [ 5 ], T5 [ 7 ] and PEGASUS [ 1 ] have dramatically advanced the fluency, coherence, and semantic richness of generated summaries. These models leverage large-scale pretraining on diverse corpora and utilize attention-based mechanisms to capture deep contextual representations. For instance, PRIMERA [ 6 ] and LongT5 [ 25 ] extend these advances to multi-document summarization by incorporating pyramid-based training strategies that prioritize salient content across multiple sources. Despite these gains, several critical limitations remain. Most transformer models use static embeddings, lacking mechanisms to adapt to temporal language dynamics, which is particularly problematic in time-sensitive domains such as news, politics, or scientific publications. Furthermore, they frequently exhibit hallucination issues—the generation of factually incorrect or unsupported content—due to the absence of factual grounding and validation modules [ 14 ] [ 26 ]. These challenges underscore the need for more context-aware and factually consistent summarization frameworks. Recent studies have also explored encoder-enhanced sequence-to-sequence pretraining strategies to improve internal representation quality in transformer architectures. For example, Zhong et al. (2024) proposed E2S2 (Encoding-Enhanced Sequence-to-Sequence Pretraining) [ 27 ], which introduces additional encoder-side self-supervised objectives, including denoising and contrastive learning, to strengthen semantic and syntactic representation learning within encoder-decoder models. Unlike conventional seq2seq pretraining approaches that primarily optimize decoder-side reconstruction objectives, E2S2 explicitly improves encoder representation quality through auxiliary supervision. Experimental results demonstrated improved performance across both natural language understanding and generation tasks. In contrast to E2S2, the proposed model in this work does not modify the underlying transformer architecture or introduce additional encoder-level pretraining objectives. Instead, our approach focuses on lightweight input-level temporal and linguistic enrichment mechanisms designed specifically for abstractive summarization under temporally evolving content. This distinction allows the proposed framework to maintain computational efficiency and compatibility with standard pretrained transformer backbones while still improving semantic adaptability and temporal contextualization. Semantic enrichment and controlled lexical augmentation To mitigate lexical rigidity and enhance semantic expressiveness, recent efforts have incorporated linguistic augmentation techniques such as synonym substitution and lexical enrichment [ 10 ] [ 28 ] [ 26 ] [ 29 ] [ 30 ] [ 31 ]. Constantin et al. [ 32 ] and Falaki & Gras [ 33 ] demonstrated that augmenting training data with WordNet-based synonym replacements improves model generalization by promoting semantic diversity and reducing overfitting to dominant vocabulary patterns. Similarly, Onan & Alhumyani [ 34 ] explored the integration of knowledge graphs and domain-specific ontologies to introduce richer contextual semantics during the summarization process. Despite their potential, these approaches are often implemented heuristically, without strict control over semantic preservation, and risk introducing semantic drift or factual inconsistencies in the generated summaries. In contrast, our proposed model applies synonym augmentation in a controlled manner, leveraging part-of-speech tagging and semantic similarity filtering to ensure that replacements maintain contextual integrity [ 35 ]. This process is empirically validated through ablation studies, allowing us to isolate and quantify the performance impact of linguistic enrichment while preserving factual consistency. However, prior studies have shown that generative transformer models may still struggle with fine-grained semantic discrimination tasks, particularly in negative paraphrase detection and subtle semantic similarity assessment. Zhong et al. (2024) [ 36 ] demonstrated that large generative language models such as ChatGPT often exhibit weaknesses in distinguishing semantically similar but logically non-equivalent sentence pairs, especially in paraphrase and similarity benchmarks. These findings suggest that lexical augmentation alone cannot fully address deep semantic reasoning limitations in generative architectures. Motivated by these observations, the proposed work treats synonym enrichment primarily as a controlled lexical diversity mechanism rather than a complete semantic understanding solution. Consequently, semantic preservation constraints and factual alignment evaluation are integrated to reduce semantic drift introduced during augmentation. Temporally-aware summarization and time-sensitive language modeling Temporally-aware summarization has emerged as a promising direction in natural language processing, particularly for domains such as news, scientific literature, and social media, where the temporal context of information plays a crucial role in determining relevance and meaning [ 10 ] [ 37 ] [ 38 ] [ 39 ] [ 40 ] [ 41 ] [ 42 ]. Shang et al. (2021) [ 43 ] introduced a temporal embedding strategy that integrates timestamp-aware representations into transformer models, enabling chronological structuring of summaries for time-sensitive corpora. Huynh-Lam et al. (2024) [ 44 ] proposed a temporal attention mechanism that dynamically emphasizes recent or time-salient content segments within the input, improving relevance in evolving information streams. Similarly, Manling et al. (2021) [ 45 ] introduced event sequencing constraints to preserve temporal coherence in multi-event narratives, particularly for summarizing news articles and legal documents. In addition to token-level temporal cues, prior work in sequence modeling [ 46 ] has shown that structural encoders can better capture long-range dependencies and preserve meaning across complex input relationships. For example, syntax-aware and graph-based encoders explicitly model structural dependencies that may be difficult to recover from shallow tagging alone. These approaches suggest that full temporal reasoning may benefit from architecture-level inductive bias rather than input-level temporal markers only. However, many of these techniques depend on access to high-fidelity timestamp metadata or require complex preprocessing pipelines such as document alignment, timeline construction, or temporal clustering; challenges that limit generalizability and scalability in real-world applications where such metadata is noisy or unavailable. In contrast, our model proposes a lightweight and scalable solution by injecting synthetic temporal cues directly into the token stream. This allows the model to internalize temporal semantics during fine-tuning without relying on explicit timestamp annotations or additional supervision, enabling efficient adaptation to temporally sensitive tasks. More recent large language model paradigms have explored prompt-based reasoning strategies, such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting [ 47 ], to improve reasoning and contextual inference capabilities. These approaches leverage intermediate reasoning steps during inference rather than modifying token representations directly. While such strategies have shown strong performance in reasoning-intensive tasks, they typically require very large instruction-tuned models and significantly higher computational overhead compared to lightweight transformer fine-tuning approaches. Factual consistency and entity-level grounding Factual consistency remains one of the most persistent and critical challenges in abstractive summarization [ 14 ] [ 48 ]. Despite producing fluent and coherent summaries, many transformer-based models frequently hallucinate facts; i.e., generate content that is either unsupported or contradicts the source document [ 49 ]. To address this issue, several training-time interventions have been proposed, including contrastive learning with entity-level supervision [ 50 ] and factual-aware objectives that penalize inconsistencies during training [ 3 ]. For example, Nan et al. (2021) [ 50 ] introduced an entity-level contrastive loss to enforce alignment between source facts and generated summaries, while Cao et al. (2020) [ 51 ] developed a post-editing mechanism using named entity recognition (NER) and replacement strategies to repair unsupported claims. Additionally, Fabbri et al. (2022) [ 52 ] proposed a QA-based evaluation framework (QAFactEval) that queries both generated and reference summaries to assess factual overlap. However, most of these methods either focus on improving generation fidelity during training or require external QA systems for evaluation, which can be computationally intensive and dataset-specific. Importantly, very few models incorporate factual alignment directly into the evaluation pipeline in a transparent and interpretable manner. In this work, we introduce a factual consistency evaluation module that uses named entity recognition to extract factual entities from both generated and reference summaries, followed by a confusion matrix analysis to quantify entity-level agreement. This provides an intuitive and diagnostic measure of factual consistency, thus bridging the gap between surface-level n -gram overlap and real-world factual alignment. Robustness and factual reasoning in related domains The challenges of robustness, compositionality, and factual grounding are not unique to summarization. Similar concerns have been tackled in Visual Question Answering (VQA), a domain that tests multimodal reasoning under adversarial conditions. Models like R-VQA [ 53 ] and ENVQA [ 54 ] have introduced mechanisms to improve robustness against visual and linguistic noise, while QSFVQA [ 55 ] emphasizes compositional reasoning in structured question answering. ESC-Net [ 56 ] defies language priors by integrating multi-layer attention filters to ensure fact-based inference. These advances echo the goals of our summarization framework: to enhance robustness against lexical and temporal variability, maintain entity-level factual grounding, and ensure interpretability in evaluation. By borrowing insights from VQA and applying them in a monomodal summarization context, we demonstrate how integrated design (i.e., not isolated enhancements) yields more robust and semantically aligned models. Evaluation and recent relevant works In terms of evaluation, most summarization studies rely on surface-level metrics like ROUGE [ 11 ], BLEU [ 12 ], METEOR [ 18 ], and BERTScore [ 19 ]. These are valuable but insufficient for a full assessment of summary quality. The proposed model includes extended quality measures such as repetition frequency, compression ratio, entity-level confusion matrices, and visualization of attention heatmaps. We further support our evaluations using statistical significance tests (ANOVA, Wilcoxon) [ 57 ] and correlation analysis (e.g., Pearson between METEOR and BLEU) [ 58 ] to ensure robustness. Temporal embeddings are trained jointly with model parameters to encode temporal signal in the embedding space, enabling contextual disambiguation of temporally dependent terms. To validate this effect, we include a temporal ablation experiment comparing Base vs + Temporal variants under identical training settings. The ablation results demonstrate consistent but moderate gains across ROUGE and METEOR metrics, supporting that the temporal cue provides auxiliary contextual signal rather than acting as a dominant feature driver. The present temporal module should therefore be interpreted as a lightweight contextual cueing mechanism rather than a complete temporal reasoning architecture. We selected this design to preserve compatibility with the underlying BART framework, maintain low computational overhead, and support reproducible CPU-efficient experimentation. We acknowledge, however, that structure-aware encoders or graph-based temporal representations may provide stronger inductive bias for modeling long-range temporal dependencies, and this constitutes an important direction for future work. We acknowledge that token-level temporal injection represents a lightweight contextual cueing strategy rather than a full temporal reasoning mechanism. More advanced reasoning-oriented approaches, including Chain-of-Thought prompting [ 47 ] and instruction-guided inference in large language models, may provide stronger long-range reasoning capabilities. However, such approaches operate under fundamentally different paradigms based on prompt engineering and large-scale instruction tuning, whereas the present work focuses on computationally efficient transformer fine-tuning for abstractive summarization. Exploring hybrid integration between temporal embedding mechanisms and reasoning-based prompting strategies remains an important direction for future research. Experiments and results This section presents the experimental design, benchmark datasets, implementation settings, and evaluation results used to assess the performance of the proposed abstractive summarization model. Comprehensive analysis is provided based on multiple performance metrics, quality indicators, training convergence, and comparative visualizations to validate the effectiveness of each model variant. Benchmark datasets To evaluate our proposed model, we utilized two widely recognized benchmark datasets in the field of abstractive summarization: XSum (Extreme Summarization) [ 15 ]: This dataset contains 225,633 news articles from the BBC, each paired with a single-sentence summary. The dataset was split into 203,028 training examples, 11,273 validation examples, and 11,332 test examples. XSum presents a challenge due to the brevity and specificity of the summaries. CNN/Daily Mail [ 16 ]: A widely used dataset for summarization tasks, consisting of 287,226 training examples, 13,367 validation examples, and 11,489 test examples. It includes news articles with multi-point bullet summaries, testing the ability to capture and summarize multi-faceted narratives. This dataset contains more extractive-style summaries compared to XSum. Comparison with well-known temporal models To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed abstractive summarization model, we conducted a comparative analysis against several well-known temporal summarization models: KETGS ( Knowledge-Enhanced Temporal Graph Summarization ) [ 34 ] introduces structured temporal and relational knowledge into the summarization process. Despite leveraging temporal graph structures, its reliance on pre-defined temporal relations and fixed knowledge graphs may limit its adaptability to nuanced semantic variations. MCHES ( Multi-Channel Hierarchical Encoder Summarizer ) [ 62 ] employs a hierarchical encoding strategy with temporal channels but lacks fine-grained temporal semantics modeling. It achieves moderate metric scores, but its output often lacks the richness and accuracy seen in more adaptive models. PEGASUS [ 1 ]: A summarization-oriented transformer pretrained with gap-sentence generation objectives and shown to achieve competitive performance on CNN/DailyMail and XSum benchmarks. BART-large [ 7 ]: A larger variant of the BART model, commonly used as a strong baseline in abstractive summarization. T5-Base [ 5 ]: A middle-sized text-to-text transfer transformer that delivers competitive generative performance with favorable efficiency trade-offs. GPT-4 [ 8 ]: Represents a powerful transformer-based model with few-shot capabilities, but when applied in a zero-shot or minimally guided summarization setting, it may underperform due to lack of domain fine-tuning or summarization-specific adaptation. Proposed Model outperforms all baselines by integrating synonym-aware augmentation and dynamic temporal embedding fusion, leading to superior semantic alignment and fidelity in generated summaries. The proposed approach was evaluated on two widely used benchmark datasets—XSum and CNN/Daily Mail—against a diverse set of baselines, including KETGS, MCHES, PEGASUS, BART large, T5 base, and GPT-4. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves consistently strong performance across multiple evaluation metrics, including BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, and BERTScore, while maintaining a balanced trade-off between lexical alignment and semantic fidelity. The ablation study confirmed the individual contributions of synonym augmentation and temporal cue integration, with the combined model variant yielding the most consistent improvements across datasets. In particular, the proposed model demonstrates enhanced performance in ROUGE-based metrics, indicating improved content coverage and structural alignment, while maintaining competitive semantic similarity compared to large language models such as GPT-4. These findings suggest that lightweight augmentation strategies, when applied in a controlled and task-aware manner, can effectively complement transformer-based summarization models. Furthermore, statistical analyses, including ANOVA, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Pearson correlation, validated that the observed improvements are statistically significant and consistent across evaluation settings. Additional qualitative and quantitative assessments indicate improvements in coverage, repetition control, and compression behavior, further supporting the robustness of the proposed approach. A practical application scenario was also explored through a document-level summarization task, where the proposed model was compared against BART large. The results demonstrated that the model can generate summaries with improved lexical alignment and contextual relevance, particularly in scenarios involving structured and time-sensitive content. This highlights the potential applicability of the approach in real-world domains such as news summarization and temporal content analysis. Despite these promising results, it is important to acknowledge several limitations. First, the temporal encoding mechanism relies on a lightweight token-based representation, which does not explicitly model complex temporal dependencies or long-range temporal reasoning. Second, the synonym augmentation strategy, although controlled, may still introduce subtle semantic variations that are not fully captured by automatic evaluation metrics. Third, the factual alignment module is used as a diagnostic evaluation tool rather than being directly integrated into the training objective, limiting its influence on model optimization. Future work will address these limitations through several directions. First, we plan to explore more expressive temporal modeling techniques, such as learnable temporal embeddings and structure-aware encoders, to better capture temporal dynamics. Second, we aim to integrate factual consistency objectives directly into the training process, potentially through contrastive learning or reinforcement learning frameworks. Third, we will investigate the use of external knowledge sources, such as Wikidata or domain-specific ontologies, to enhance factual grounding and reduce hallucinations. Additionally, extending the model to multilingual and cross-lingual settings will improve its applicability across diverse linguistic and temporal contexts. Finally, we aim to further position the proposed approach within the evolving landscape of large language models by conducting broader evaluations that combine fine-tuned models with instruction-based systems. In this context, the proposed model can be viewed as a complementary approach that provides controlled, efficient, and interpretable summarization, particularly in scenarios where domain adaptation and reproducibility are essential. In conclusion, this work contributes an effective and practical enhancement to transformer-based abstractive summarization by integrating temporal awareness and controlled lexical enrichment. While not introducing a fundamentally new architecture, the proposed approach demonstrates that carefully designed augmentations can lead to consistent and meaningful improvements, opening pathways for more robust and adaptable summarization systems in real-world applications.
