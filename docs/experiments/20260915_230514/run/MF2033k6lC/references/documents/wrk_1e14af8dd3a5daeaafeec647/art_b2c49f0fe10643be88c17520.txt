Recent progress in fMRI-based visual brain decoding has enabled the reconstruction of neural visual signals as high-quality images using diffusion-based generative models. However, evaluating the semantic fidelity of these reconstructions remains challenging. Existing metrics, primarily based on pixel-level similarity or global embeddings, fail to capture the saliency-driven, structural, and relational aspects central to human scene understanding. We propose a saliency-aware, graph-based similarity metric, SSG, that compares stimuli and reconstructions through compact semantic scene graphs centered on the most salient objects and their relationships. By integrating visual saliency, language-guided scene understanding, and instance-level grounding, our method produces weighted graph representations that emphasize meaningful content while suppressing background clutter. Reconstruction quality is assessed via saliency-weighted object and relation conformity, yielding an interpretable structural score aligned with human perceptual judgment. Experiments show that the proposed metric provides a more informative evaluation of visual brain reconstructions than existing alternatives and offers diagnostic insights that can guide reconstruction refinement.
